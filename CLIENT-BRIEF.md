## In one paragraph

StellaXafe is a private AI assistant built for your company. Your staff ask
for a document in normal language. The assistant already knows your files,
asks a few short questions, confirms what it understood, and produces the
finished work in your format. Every fact points back to its source so a
person can check it. Your customers can ask it questions on WhatsApp, and
it answers from your approved information only. Your data stays inside your
own private cloud, and nothing is used to train a model.

## The problem you already have

Your best people are already using AI. The good ones got there first.

To get useful work out of a public chatbot, they paste in what makes the
work good: a client file, a contract, a price list, last year's report. A
tool that does not know your company gives weak work, and the deadline is
today. So they paste it in. Nobody was careless. The job needed the context.

Once that happens, you cannot see it, you cannot take it back, and no
contract covers it. A policy does not survive a deadline.

The second cost is quieter. Staff write a prompt, get something close, and
rewrite it. Then again. Each round costs time, and the result still does not
sound like your firm.

StellaXafe gives your staff the quality they were chasing, without the
paste.

## What your staff experience

1. They ask for what they need, the way they would ask a colleague.
2. The assistant asks three to five questions. Only the ones it cannot
   answer from your own files.
3. It reads the request back: what it will make, who it is for, which of
   your documents it will use. The staff member fixes anything wrong.
4. Only then does it write.
5. The finished file is saved to your own storage, and a download link
   appears in the chat.

Nobody learns prompt writing. The questions do that job, and because the
assistant already knows your company, there are few of them.

## It knows your company

Your admin approves the files the assistant may read: templates, past
projects, price lists, standards, policies. When it writes, it uses your
formats, your numbers and your wording. Staff do not paste any of it in.

Nothing is trained on your data. The assistant reads an approved file when
it needs it. It does not absorb the file into a shared model, so nothing you
upload can turn up in anyone else's answer.

## You can check every answer

The assistant does not fetch information and dump it on you.

1. It gathers the information.
2. It checks it, and marks anything it could not confirm.
3. It shows you the source of each fact, as a file or a link, so you can
   open it and verify it in seconds.

If something is missing, it says so and tells you who in your company to ask
or where to look. It does not guess to fill the gap. Nothing unconfirmed
enters a document until a person approves it.

This matters where a wrong figure costs money or credibility: a load
calculation, a contract clause, a regulatory filing.

## Your data stays protected

- **Each company is separate.** Your workspace is yours. Database rules stop
  one company from ever seeing another's records.
- **Each staff member is separate.** One person's chats and notes are never
  shown to another.
- **Files are encrypted** in storage and while they travel.
- **Nothing you do trains a model.** Your chats and files stay yours.
- **It runs in your own private cloud.** We build it inside a private AWS
  account for your company, in a VPC (a network space only your company
  uses). The assistant reaches the AI model over a private link inside that
  network, so that traffic does not cross the public internet.
- **Your admin controls what it can read.** A file that is not approved does
  not exist as far as the assistant is concerned.

## Your files stay with you

The software does not become another place your data lives. It reads from
where you already keep files, and it saves finished work back to the same
place.

Each company gets its own private AWS account, locked to that company, and
your files are stored there. Staff can also save a finished file straight to
their own Google Drive.

## It fits the tools you already use

The assistant connects to the systems your team works in, so nobody has to
move. Examples are Google Drive, Gmail, Google Workspace, Slack and Notion.
We agree the exact list with you at the start, based on what you use.

## Research from the internet, when you want it

If a task needs outside information, the assistant can research it online.
You stay in charge.

1. It names the sites it wants to look at and asks first.
2. It finds the details it needs.
3. It shows you those details with the link to each, and asks again before
   any of it goes into your document.

You never open a finished document and find data you did not approve.

## Your customers can use it on WhatsApp

The assistant also lives where your customers already are. They message your
WhatsApp number, and the assistant answers.

- **It answers only from what you approved**, and only about your business.
  If your admin has not put it in, the assistant does not know it.
- **It is a front desk.** Customers can ask questions. They cannot upload
  files, change what the assistant knows, or ask it to do staff work.
- **A person can step in at any time.** If a customer asks for a human, or
  asks something the assistant should not answer alone, it says so in the
  same chat and your team is notified. Your team replies from the place they
  already answer from. While they are in the conversation, the assistant
  stays quiet, and it comes back after a few minutes of silence.

Your customers get an instant answer at any hour. Your staff only see the
conversations that need a person.

## You are not locked to one AI model

The assistant is provider agnostic. It can run on the model that suits you,
and you can change models later without rebuilding. Your files, your rules
and your workflow stay as they are.

## What you get back

- **Fewer retries.** The back and forth happens in a short chat before the
  document exists.
- **Less time per document.** Staff start from a request, not a blank page.
- **The same standard from everyone.** Your templates and your wording, no
  matter who asks.
- **An answer for your own clients.** When a client or auditor asks where
  your data goes, you can tell them.

## Where it fits

**Engineering firms.** Progress reports, tender responses, method
statements, inspection reports. Figures come from your own project files and
each one points to its source.

**Law firms.** First drafts of letters, memos and agreements in your house
style, built from your precedents. Client files stay inside your boundary,
and every reference can be checked.

**Any firm whose files cannot go into a public chatbot.**

## What it costs to run

You pay for what you use, straight to AWS, on your own account. There is no
seat fee and no per user charge.

- **Storage.** About 2 US cents per GB per month. 100 GB of company files
  costs about $2.30 a month.
- **The AI model.** You pay for usage only, billed by AWS each month. The
  cost follows how much your team uses it and which model you choose. You
  set a monthly budget and get an alert before you reach it.
- **The private network link.** A small fixed charge, about $15 a month,
  for the connection that keeps your traffic off the public internet.
- **Starting credit.** A new AWS account comes with up to $200 in AWS
  credits, which covers the first months of running costs.

## Support and maintenance

- **The first six months of maintenance are included.** Fixes, updates and
  adjustments as your team settles in.
- **After that, you choose.** A maintenance review every three months, or a
  retainer agreed with you, where I look after the system as part of your
  team.

## The AWS numbers (for Anthony)

Researched 2026-10-31. Sources at the end.

**The free offer is 6 months now, not 12.** AWS changed it on 15 July 2025.
A new account gets $100 in credits at sign up, and up to $100 more for
trying certain services. Up to $200 in total.

**Do not leave the client on the Free plan.** A new account picks a Free
plan or a Paid plan. Both get the credits. But the Free plan ends after 6
months or when the credits run out, and then AWS suspends the account. If it
is not upgraded within 90 days, AWS erases the account and everything in it.
The Free plan also blocks some services. So: open the client's account on the
Paid plan from day one. The credits still apply to the bills. AWS's own FAQ
says in one place the credits expire 6 months after the account opens, and in
another 12 months. Check the date in the account's billing console.

**Storage (S3 Standard).** $0.023 per GB per month for the first 50 TB.
10 GB is about 23 cents a month, not a few cents per 10 GB. Still small.
100 GB is about $2.30 a month. Requests and data going out of AWS are
charged separately, and are small at this size.

**The model (Bedrock).** Paid per use, on the AWS bill, every month. There
is no package of prepaid credits to buy on Bedrock itself. The AWS credits
above can cover it. If a client ever uses a provider directly instead
(Anthropic or DeepSeek), that is where prepaid credit packs exist. Example
rate from a third party guide, not AWS: Claude Sonnet at $3 per million
tokens in and $15 per million tokens out (a token is a piece of a word,
roughly three quarters of one). At that rate a long document that reads 50
pages of company files and writes 10 pages costs roughly 20 to 50 US cents,
more if it goes through several rounds. That is why the client text says
"follows usage" and names no per document figure. Check the Bedrock pricing
page for the exact model before quoting anything.

**PrivateLink (the private network link).** About $0.01 per hour for each
availability zone (a separate data centre inside one AWS region) the
endpoint runs in, plus $0.01 per GB that passes through. Two zones, which is
the normal setup for reliability, is about $14.60 a month before data. This
is the one fixed monthly cost, so it is in the client text.

**Maintenance.** Written as you said: six months included, then a review
every three months or a retainer. I read "we cannot discuss a retainer" as
"we can discuss a retainer". Tell me if you meant otherwise.
